About This Reaction Time Game

Reaction Games measures the delay between a visual signal and your response. Each test uses five randomized rounds so you cannot predict exactly when the green signal will appear. Your browser calculates every result locally in milliseconds.

Reaction speed can change with focus, fatigue, device input delay, and practice. Treat the score as a fun personal benchmark rather than a medical assessment.

Tips for a Faster Reaction Time

  • Use the same device when comparing personal scores.
  • Keep your hand relaxed and ready before each signal.
  • Focus on the center indicator instead of the surrounding page.
  • Take a short break if your times become slower across repeated tests.

Reaction Games FAQ

What is a good reaction time?

Many desktop users record visual reaction times between roughly 200 and 300 milliseconds, but results vary by person, device, display, browser, and input method.

Can I play this reaction game on mobile?

Yes. The game supports touch controls and adapts to smaller screens. Tap the large game area when it turns green.

Why did I get a false start?

A false start happens when you tap, click, or press Space before the green signal appears. The same round restarts, so the early tap does not affect your average.

Does the game save my score?

Your best reaction time is saved locally in your browser. It is not sent to a server and may disappear if you clear browser storage.
